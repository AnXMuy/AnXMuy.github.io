Balancing research with coursework works better when the week is planned around momentum rather than around perfect daily symmetry. I try to reserve the highest-focus blocks for tasks that are hard to resume midstream, such as paper reading, experiment interpretation, or writing sections that require tight logic.

For longer projects, I break work into units that can produce visible progress within a few days. That usually means one concrete experiment, one cleaned note, or one clarified figure instead of a vague goal like "push the project forward." Small deliverables make it easier to recover after interruptions from classes or deadlines.

The key is consistency. Undergraduate schedules are noisy, so the workflow has to survive imperfect weeks. A process that only works in ideal conditions is not actually a workflow; it is a wish.
